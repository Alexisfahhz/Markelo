# Markelo, Capture Sheet and Synthesis Method

**Why this document exists:** Many people will do interviews in different places. If everyone writes notes in their own format, we get anecdotes instead of evidence. Everyone must use the same capture sheet. This makes the results comparable.

## Part 2, Current-state observation checklist

Use this when you can watch the process or see the room. **Observation beats interview.** People describe their own process inaccurately. Paper does not.

**Physical script journey, record what you see**
- Where are blank booklets stored before the exam?
- Who carries scripts from the exam hall? In what container?
- Where do scripts sit between collection and marking? Is it locked?
- How are scripts counted? Is there a signed handover record?
- How are scripts grouped, by course, by hall, by department?

**Photograph these if permitted**
1. A blank answer booklet, front and back
2. Any existing cover sheet or attendance sheet
3. A completed mark sheet
4. The scanner or photocopier, including the model label
5. The result portal upload screen
6. The room where scripts are stored

**Count these**
- Pages in a typical booklet
- Booklets in a typical bundle
- Staff involved from collection to result

**Ask about the exception, not the normal case**
- What happens when a student uses a second booklet?
- What happens when a script has no name on it?
- What happens when a script is found after results are released?
- What happens when a marker becomes unavailable halfway through?

## Part 3, Weekly synthesis method

Do this every week during the campaign. Do not wait until the end.

**Step 1, Collect.** Put all capture sheets in one place.

**Step 2, Count the assumption results.** For each assumption ID, count Supported / Contradicted / Still unknown.

**Step 3, Apply the evidence rule.**
- **1 source** = a signal. Write it down. Change nothing.
- **3 or more independent sources that agree** = evidence. You may change the design.
- **Any contradiction** = stop and investigate. A single strong contradiction is more valuable than five weak confirmations.

**Step 4, Update the assumption map.** Move rows between tiers. Record the date and the evidence.

**Step 5, Write the weekly summary.** Three items only:
1. What we learned that we did not know last week.
2. What this changes in the product.
3. What we still cannot answer.

**Step 6, Report the numbers honestly.** Say "4 of 7 exam officers said X". Never say "exam officers say X" when you spoke to four people.

## Common mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it damages the research |
|---|---|
| Describing Markelo before asking questions | The person agrees to be polite. The answer becomes worthless. |
| Asking "would you use this?" | People say yes and never buy. Ask what they do now instead. |
| Only interviewing friendly contacts | Friendly people agree with you. Find people who will say no. |
| Writing notes days later | You will remember your conclusion, not their words. |
| Counting one enthusiastic person as proof | One person is a signal, not evidence. |
| Skipping the ICT director | This person can block the sale. |
